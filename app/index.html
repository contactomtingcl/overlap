Overlap · two satellites, one honest burning calendar

25 years of NASA FIRMS fire hotspots from MODIS (coarse pixels, since 2000) and VIIRS (fine pixels, since 2012), harmonized into one comparable series for Chile. Every estimate shows how much to trust it.

Burning activity calendar

The 2012 jump, before and after

When VIIRS arrives in 2012 it sees many more small fires than MODIS. Joined as they are, the record seems to explode overnight. Harmonized, every year speaks the same unit.

How much to trust these numbers

Joining two sensors is never exact. We measured the error on years the model never saw (2020–2026) and against 75,718 CONAF fire records (2002–2020).

How to read this

No detection is not the same as no fire. Each polar satellite sees a place only about twice a day, and clouds, smoke and satellite gaps hide fires. Hatched cells mean the record could not be built for that month, not zero fire.

Hotspots are not fires. One large fire produces many hotspots; small agricultural or short-lived burns may produce none.
